# Local relation duality convention

For the cohomological variation

\[
 V=R^{2n-1}\pi_*\mathbf Q(n),
\]

the degree-one full-support intermediate-extension stalk at a nodal fiber is
the extra **cohomology** space

\[
 E(Y_p)=\mathcal H^{-d+1}(IC(V))_p.
\]

The homological vanishing-cycle relation space is

\[
 R(Y_p)=\ker\!\left(\bigoplus_yH_{2n-1}(Z_{y,\infty},\mathbf Q(n))
 \to H_{2n-1}(Y_\infty,\mathbf Q(n))\right).
\]

Saito's exact sequence supplies the canonical perfect pairing

\[
 E(Y_p)\times R(Y_p)\to\mathbf Q,
 \qquad E(Y_p)=R(Y_p)^\vee.
\]

Accordingly, phrases in the earlier local-arrangement bricks saying that an
IC channel “is the relation kernel” are kernel models after polarization and
compatible Picard-Lefschetz orientation. Intrinsically:

- the cohomological stalk is \(R(Y_p)^\vee\);
- its dual/homological model is \(R(Y_p)\);
- ranks, vanishing, and pure Tate type are unchanged by duality;
- a specified local incidence class is a functional on relations, never a
  canonically selected relation vector.

B134 is authoritative for class-level use. Its formula is

\[
 s_m(\zeta)_p(\beta)=\langle\zeta,\gamma_\beta\rangle.
\]

NG107 forbids using a nonzero relation space as proof that this functional is
nonzero.

In the normal-crossing residue model of B135, write
\(\operatorname{Res}_{D_i}\nabla\widetilde\nu=a_i\delta_i\). Then

\[
 E(Y_p)=\operatorname{coker}\Delta^\ast,\qquad
 R(Y_p)=\ker\Delta,
\]

with pairing \(\langle[a],b\rangle=\sum_i a_i b_i\). A change of lift adds
\(\Delta^\ast(v)\), so individual residue coordinates are not intrinsic. If
\(\delta_2=c\delta_1\), the invariant coordinate is \(c a_1-a_2\).
