# B155 — Smooth excess is an all-order rank factorization

Let

\[
 \tau:(W,0)\longrightarrow(\mathbf C^N,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}d\tau_0=R<N,
\]

and let \(I_\tau\) be the ideal generated by its components. The following
are equivalent.

1. **Smooth reduced excess.** The analytic scheme defined by \(I_\tau\) is
   reduced and smooth of codimension \(R\).
2. **Zero Kuranishi germ.** B154's reduced map

   \[
   \kappa:(\ker d\tau_0,0)\longrightarrow
   (\operatorname{coker}d\tau_0,0)
   \]

   vanishes identically.
3. **Rank-\(R\) factorization.** There are an analytic submersion

   \[
   f:(W,0)\longrightarrow(\mathbf C^R,0)
   \]

   and an analytic matrix \(A:(W,0)\to\operatorname{Mat}_{N\times R}\)
   with \(\operatorname{rank}A(0)=R\) such that

   \[
   \tau=A\,f.
   \]

## Proof of the equivalence

Assume 3. An \(R\times R\) minor of \(A\) is a unit. Selecting those rows
and inverting the minor gives an analytic left inverse \(B\) with
\(BA=I_R\). Hence

\[
 f=B\tau,\qquad (\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N)=(f_1,\ldots,f_R).
\]

Because \(f\) is a submersion, its zero scheme is reduced and smooth of
codimension \(R\). Thus 3 implies 1.

Assume 1. In the regular analytic local ring of \(W\), the ideal of the
smooth codimension-\(R\) germ is generated by part of a regular coordinate
system \(f_1,\ldots,f_R\). Since this ideal equals \(I_\tau\), there are
analytic matrices \(A,B\) with

\[
 \tau=A f,\qquad f=B\tau.
\]

Differentiating the first identity at the origin gives
\(d\tau_0=A(0)df_0\). Both \(d\tau_0\) and \(df_0\) have rank \(R\), so
\(\operatorname{rank}A(0)=R\). Thus 1 implies 3.

To compare with 2, solve \(R\) independent components of \(\tau\) as in
B154 and use them as normal coordinates \(y\). In the resulting coordinates,

\[
 \tau=(y,h(v,y)),\qquad h(v,0)=\kappa(v).
\]

If \(\kappa\equiv0\), the analytic Hadamard lemma gives
\(h(v,y)=D(v,y)y\), so

\[
 \tau=
 \begin{pmatrix}I_R\\D\end{pmatrix}y,
\]

which is 3. Conversely, 3 makes the zero scheme \(f=0\), so implicit
reduction leaves the zero Kuranishi map. Hence 2 and 3 are equivalent.

## No finite-order substitute

For every \(m\ge3\), take two local ordinary-double-point charts with fixed
spatial quadratic form \(q(z)\) and critical values

\[
 \tau_m(x,y)=(x,x+y^m).
\]

This germ has differential rank \(R=1<N=2\). Its reduced Kuranishi map is

\[
 \kappa_m(y)=y^m.
\]

All tensors of degrees \(<m\) vanish, but

\[
 I_{\tau_m}=(x,y^m)
\]

is nonreduced. For any prescribed finite verification order \(k\), choosing
\(m>k\) gives the same \(k\)-jet as the integrable germ
\(\tau_\infty=(x,x)\), yet it fails every equivalent all-order condition
above.

More generally, the first nonzero homogeneous Kuranishi term is invariant
under analytic source and target coordinate changes with the canonical
linear parts. Thus an order-by-order programme yields genuine obstructions
but can never certify integration after finitely many steps.
