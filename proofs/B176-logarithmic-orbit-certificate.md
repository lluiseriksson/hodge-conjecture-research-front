# B176 — Logarithmic-orbit certificate for \(H_\tau=0\)

Let \((M,0)\) be a smooth complex analytic germ of dimension \(d\), let

\[
 \tau=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N),\qquad
 I=I_\tau=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N),
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}d\tau_0=R,
\]

and define the labelled ideal-preserving derivations

\[
 \Theta(-\log I)
 =\{\delta\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbf C}(\mathcal O_{M,0}):
          \delta(I)\subseteq I\}.
\]

Every such derivation evaluates into the Zariski tangent kernel:

\[
 \operatorname{ev}_0\Theta(-\log I)
 \subseteq \ker d\tau_0. \tag{1}
\]

Indeed, \(\delta(\tau_i)\in I\subset\mathfrak m\), so evaluation at the
origin gives

\[
 d\tau_i(0)(\delta(0))=0
\]

for every \(i\).

## The equivalence

The following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(H_\tau=0\).
2. \(\operatorname{ev}_0\Theta(-\log I)=\ker d\tau_0\).
3. There are \(d-R\) derivations
   \(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_{d-R}\in\Theta(-\log I)\) whose values at
   the origin are linearly independent.
4. The local pseudogroup generated by ideal-preserving holomorphic flows
   has an orbit of dimension \(d-R\) through the origin.

By B156, condition 1 gives submersive coordinates

\[
 (u_1,\ldots,u_R,v_1,\ldots,v_{d-R})
\]

such that \(I=(u_1,\ldots,u_R)\). Then every
\(\partial_{v_j}\) preserves \(I\), and their values form a basis of
\(\ker d\tau_0\). This proves 1 implies 2, while (1) makes conditions 2
and 3 equivalent. The local flows of the vector fields in condition 3
give condition 4.

It remains to prove the converse. Choose \(R\) constant linear
combinations \(f_1,\ldots,f_R\) of the \(\tau_i\) with independent
differentials at the origin. Then

\[
 F=V(f_1,\ldots,f_R)
\]

is a smooth germ of dimension \(d-R\), and

\[
 V(I)\subseteq F. \tag{2}
\]

If \(\delta(I)\subseteq I\), its local flow preserves \(V(I)\). To see
this without assuming reducedness, choose generators \(g\) of \(I\) and
write \(\delta(g)=A g\). Along a flow line starting in \(V(I)\), the
vector \(g\) solves a homogeneous analytic linear ODE with zero initial
value, hence stays zero.

Under condition 4, the orbit \(S\) through the origin is therefore a
smooth \((d-R)\)-dimensional germ satisfying

\[
 S\subseteq V(I)\subseteq F.
\]

The inclusion \(S\hookrightarrow F\) has an isomorphism on tangent spaces
at the origin. The inverse-function theorem makes \(S\) an open germ of
\(F\), so \(V(I)=F\) as germs. Every \(\tau_i\) vanishes on the smooth
germ \(F\), whose radical ideal is exactly
\((f_1,\ldots,f_R)\). Thus

\[
 I=(f_1,\ldots,f_R).
\]

It has \(R\) minimal generators, and B156 gives \(H_\tau=0\). This proves
4 implies 1 and completes the equivalence.

## Exact scope

B176 does not derive the required vector fields from freeness of the
reduced product \(\prod_i\tau_i\). It replaces G100 by an equivalent
geometric construction target that retains the labelled ideal itself.
The full Hodge route must still construct this logarithmic orbit in the
complete-linear-system germ and retain every class-specific detector
clause.
