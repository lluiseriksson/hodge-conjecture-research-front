# B252 — Two dimensions beyond slope-eight equality are impossible

Assume the standard polarization and

\[
 \dim S=h_Z(1)=5d-1,\qquad d\ge10. \tag{1}
\]

Choose a nonorthogonal marked pair \(v,w\), put

\[
 U=\langle v,w\rangle^\perp, \tag{2}
\]

and use B231 to exclude the all-orthogonal alternative.

## The residual branch falls below B246

Suppose every other marked point lies in \(U\). Modulo
\(T_v\oplus T_w\), the residual point span has dimension at most

\[
 (5d-1)-(2d+2)=3d-3. \tag{3}
\]

The direct decomposition
\(V=\langle v,w\rangle\perp U\) identifies the quotient generated by
the residual squares with their standard quadratic point span in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\); the projected full tangents are exactly the
standard tangent osculators on \(Q(U)\). The residual quadric has
dimension \(D=d-2\ge8\), and (3) is \(3D+3\). Hence the
linear-algebraic standard-polarization floor in B246 applies and
requires dimension at least

\[
 5D-3=5d-13>3d-3. \tag{4}
\]

This is impossible.

## The rank-one escape test

We use the following consequence of the self-adjoint model repeatedly.
Suppose \(J\subset V\) has dimension \(d-3\) and
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2J\) is contained in the current annihilator.
For every \(z\in J\), the rank-one self-adjoint map

\[
 E_z(y)=B(z,y)z \tag{5}
\]

belongs to that annihilator. A common eigenvector of all \(E_z\) lies
in \(J^\perp\): if \(x\notin J^\perp\), one can choose
\(z\in J\), not proportional to \(x\), with \(B(z,x)\ne0\), and then
\(E_zx\notin\mathbf Cx\). Thus a filled span would confine every
marked point to
\(\mathbf P(J^\perp)\simeq\mathbf P^4\), whose quadratic point rank is
at most fifteen.

If the span is not yet filled, the point-rank bound supplies a marked
\(x\notin J^\perp\). Contraction of
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2J\) at \(x\) has image \(J\); after quotienting
by \(\mathbf Cx\), its rank is at least

\[
 \dim J-1=d-4. \tag{6}
\]

## A third point meets the hyperbolic plane

Choose \(r\notin U\) and put

\[
 S_0=T_v+T_w+T_r,\qquad
 R=\langle v,w,r\rangle,\qquad W=R^\perp. \tag{7}
\]

B237 gives \(\dim S_0=3d+2\). Choose a marked \(t\notin R\).

### The case \(t\notin W\)

B246 gives

\[
 S_1=S_0+T_t,\qquad \dim S_1=4d+1,\qquad
 S_1^\perp=\operatorname{Sym}^2K. \tag{8}
\]

where \(K=W\cap t^\perp\) has dimension \(d-2\). Its contact locus is
\(K^\perp\), of quadratic point rank at most ten, so choose a marked
\(u\notin K^\perp\).

Contraction of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\) at \(u\), modulo
\(\mathbf Cu\), has rank \(d-2\) if \(u\notin K\) and \(d-3\) if
\(u\in K\).

If \(u\notin K\), the tangent fills (1). Put
\(J=K\cap u^\perp\). Then \(\dim J=d-3\), and all the rank-one maps
(5) remain in the annihilator. Every later contact point lies in
\(\mathbf P(J^\perp)\simeq\mathbf P^4\), whose quadratic point rank is
at most fifteen, contradicting (1).

If \(u\in K\), only \(d-3\) dimensions are added and one dimension
remains. The same \(J\) and the same rank-one maps survive. Since the
marked point rank exceeds fifteen, choose a marked
\(x\notin J^\perp\). Contraction of
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2J\) at \(x\), modulo \(\mathbf Cx\), has rank at
least

\[
 \dim J-1=d-4>1. \tag{9}
\]

contradicting the last one-dimensional quotient.

### The case \(t\in W\)

Now

\[
 S_1=S_0+T_t,\qquad \dim S_1=4d,\qquad
 L=S_1^\perp=
 \{A\in\operatorname{Sym}^2W:At\in\mathbf Ct\}. \tag{10}
\]

Its contact locus is \(R\cup\mathbf Ct\). Choose a marked \(u\)
outside it and put \(K=t^\perp\cap W\), of dimension \(d-2\).

If \(u\in K^\perp\), B245 shows that \(T_u\) contributes exactly one
dimension and leaves annihilator \(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\). Choose a
marked \(x\notin K^\perp\). If \(x\notin K\), its contribution
\(d-2\) fills (1); if \(x\in K\), its contribution \(d-3\) leaves one
dimension. In both cases \(J=K\cap x^\perp\), \(\dim J=d-3\), supplies
the surviving maps (5). The filled case gives the projective-four-space
contradiction, and the one-dimensional case is contradicted by (9).

It remains to take

\[
 u\notin K^\perp. \tag{11}
\]

If \(u\notin K\), contraction of
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\subset L\) contributes \(d-2\) dimensions.
Thus either the full tangent already exceeds the \(d-1\) available
dimensions, or at most one dimension remains. With
\(J=K\cap u^\perp\), the rank-one maps (5) survive, and the same
filled-or-escape argument gives a contradiction.

Finally suppose \(u\in K\). The subspace
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\subset L\) contributes \(d-3\) dimensions.
Put \(J=K\cap u^\perp\), again of dimension \(d-3\). Every \(E_z\)
for \(z\in J\) annihilates \(T_u\). If the full tangent exceeds the
available \(d-1\) dimensions there is an immediate contradiction;
otherwise at most

\[
 (d-1)-(d-3)=2. \tag{12}
\]

dimensions remain. A marked \(x\notin J^\perp\) exists by the
fifteen-dimensional point-rank bound, and its residual tangent rank is
at least

\[
 d-4>2. \tag{13}
\]

again impossible.

Every standard branch at (1) is excluded. B249 already excludes the
square and every higher polarization at this rank. Thus G175 and the
adjacent odd layer \(s=8d-3\) fail on the valid even quadrics
\(Q^d\), \(d\ge10\). The next balanced signature is

\[
 s=8d-2,\qquad \delta_1=4d-1,\qquad
 N=10d,\qquad h_Z(1)=5d=N/2. \tag{14}
\]

B252 is a necessary special-input obstruction. It constructs no
configuration, ODP package, rational detector, specified pairing,
algebraic cycle, proof, or disproof of HC.
